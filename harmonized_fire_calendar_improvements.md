# Improvements Only — MODIS/VIIRS Fire Harmonization Brief

## P0 — Correct the scientific and factual foundation

1. **Remove or substantiate the claimed Suomi-NPP shutdown.** As of the research snapshot (2026-11-10), NASA FIRMS still lists Suomi-NPP alongside NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. Do not hard-code a November 2, 2026 cutover or promise a 33% discontinuity correction without an authoritative mission notice and verified product-availability records. Drive sensor availability from the FIRMS availability metadata and expose the last observed date per sensor. [NASA FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/)

2. **Replace the “14-stage physics” assertion with a small, evidence-backed baseline.** Several proposed corrections (fixed spectral coefficient, LAI attenuation, angle multipliers, cloud-obscuration floor, fixed persistence thresholds, and QA bit assignments) are presented as physical facts without product/version references or fitted evidence. For each correction, either cite the exact product guide and validate it, or remove it from the MVP. Decode QA fields by product and collection; do not assume one generic 32-bit mask or apply it to FIRMS CSV fields that do not contain it. [NASA FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)

3. **Define exactly what the harmonized value estimates.** Separate “number of detections,” “cell-day with ≥1 detection,” “fire-episode count,” “FRP,” and “burned area.” They answer different questions and are not interchangeable. Make one primary estimand and reference scale explicit (for example, observed cell-day fire presence on a fixed grid, calibrated to a stated reference sensor); keep other metrics clearly labeled as secondary.

4. **Do not call active-fire detections burned area or infer area directly from FRP.** Active-fire detections, fire radiative power, and burned-area products have different meanings, spatial/temporal resolutions, and error modes. If burned area is included, use a verified burned-area product with its own product/version provenance and validation; remove the “FIRMS VIIRS Global Burned Area” wording unless an authoritative catalog confirms that exact product and relationship. [NASA FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)

5. **Correct the GDELT historical-coverage claim.** The proposed `gdeltv2.gkg_partitioned` query cannot substantiate a complete 2000–2026 news cube. GDELT’s own data page describes the relevant GKG-scale archive in the 2015 era; inspect actual table partition bounds and source coverage before making any date-range claim. Mark unavailable years explicitly and use a separate, documented source for earlier periods rather than implying GDELT 2.0 provides them. [GDELT data and documentation](https://www.gdeltproject.org/data.html)

6. **Treat missing news as “no match found,” never as evidence of a silent ignition or sensor artifact.** News coverage varies by language, outlet, access, geocoding, and time. A satellite anomaly with no retrieved article is not proof of an unreported fire; no article plus a corrected sensor series is not proof that an artifact was fixed. Replace categorical statuses with evidence-aware wording, source counts, search coverage, and calibrated confidence; keep “possible event” separate from “verified event.”

7. **Remove fabricated-looking example outputs until they are reproduced from source data.** Values such as exact report counts, anomaly sigma/percentiles, regime percentages, trend p-values, dates, and “zero step-change” should be generated by the pipeline and linked to an inspectable run. Label mock data as synthetic in the UI and prevent it from being confused with NASA observations.

## P1 — Make the harmonization defensible and testable

8. **Calibrate from co-observed data rather than hand-picked ratios or presumed physics.** Use sensor-overlap periods to construct spatially and temporally matched observations, stratified at minimum by biome/land cover, day/night, scan/view angle, and quality. Fit a transparent baseline first; only add more complex models if held-out results improve. Do not describe distribution matching as guaranteeing physical equivalence: quantile mapping can align marginal distributions while distorting trends, extremes, or conditional relationships.

9. **Aggregate to a common support and define duplicate/episode rules before comparing sensors.** MODIS and VIIRS have different and angle-dependent footprints and overpass schedules. Prefer a documented common grid and cell-time aggregation, with sensitivity checks for grid size and time window. Distinguish same-sensor repeated observations, cross-sensor co-observations, and separate fire episodes; avoid summing detections as though each were a unique fire.

10. **Normalize by observing opportunity and report data gaps.** Incorporate valid observation coverage, cloud/quality flags, overpass availability, and sensor presence into denominators or uncertainty. Do not interpret “no detected fire” as “no fire” when the cell was unobserved or cloud-obscured. Replace a fixed cloud floor or a claimed zero discontinuity with measured coverage and residual uncertainty.

11. **Add a real validation protocol before claiming continuity.** Split calibration and evaluation by both time and geography; report held-out bias, MAE/RMSE, rank/seasonal agreement, event-detection precision/recall where reference labels exist, and residual discontinuity around sensor transitions. Include raw-vs-harmonized plots, ablations, uncertainty intervals, and failure cases. Test against a simple baseline so added complexity proves its value.

12. **Use uncertainty and coverage labels as first-class outputs.** Show raw observation, calibrated estimate, confidence/interval, source sensors, quality filters, and observation coverage together. Mark partial years, unavailable sensor periods, and interpolated/forecast values distinctly; do not silently interpolate a future or incomplete year into an apparently observed calendar.

13. **Make every coefficient and output reproducible.** Version the input products, collection, retrieval date, processing code, spatial/temporal aggregation, calibration parameters, training/evaluation splits, and output schema. Publish a small manifest and a deterministic command that regenerates the demo results. Add tests for boundary dates, missing sensors, leap years, ISO week 1/52/53 transitions, antimeridian AOIs, and all-zero/low-count cells.

14. **Make the proposed optimal-transport implementation safe and statistically valid.** Validate non-empty, equal-length, finite, monotonic quantile arrays; interpolate between quantile bins; define behavior above/below training support; and use a held-out distribution rather than claiming exact equivalence from 100 bins. The example function’s binary search index is not itself an empirical CDF estimate and currently has no validation for malformed arrays or mismatched distributions.

## P1 — Focus the product on a compelling, reliable demo

15. **Cut the first release to one reliable end-to-end story.** Prioritize NASA data ingestion → transparent harmonization → one focused calendar/map → evidence/provenance export. Defer the full multilingual RAG, MCP server, multi-agent UI controller, n8n automation, burned-area coupling, and many optional grids until the core result is validated. The challenge page should be checked against its exact required deliverables and judging criteria before committing to this scope; its supplied URL could not be retrieved during this research run.

16. **Replace speculative performance promises with a tested data plan.** Measure the actual data volume, query latency, and browser memory for a documented AOI and global summary. NASA GIBS is an imagery service, not a substitute for querying or harmonizing active-fire records. For cloud-native access, reuse proven formats/catalog patterns (STAC + GeoParquet, partition/row-group pruning, sorted temporal/spatial keys) and document any API-key, CORS, HTTP Range, or rate-limit constraints; do not promise a sub-180 MB archive or <100 ms end-to-end analysis without measurements. [NASA GIBS API](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software/earthdata-developer-portal/gibs-api) · [FIRMS GeoParquet catalog](https://github.com/portolan-mirrors/firms-catalog)

17. **Simplify the spatial and temporal choices.** Pick one primary aggregation grid/resolution and one useful calendar view for the demo. Explain the grid’s ground scale and sensitivity; H3 resolution 7/8 and 0.25° are not automatically interchangeable or suitable for every AOI. Make monthly/weekly bins use explicit calendar definitions and test year-boundary behavior before advertising daily, ISO-week, pentad, and diurnal views.

18. **Design the evidence panel around scientific interpretation, not AI theater.** On selecting a cell, show raw records, sensors, timestamps, confidence/quality, overpass context, calibrated value, uncertainty, and linked sources. If AI narration remains, constrain it to summarizing these computed fields with citations; it must not calculate measurements, invent a ground-truth label, or hide failed/missing sources. Avoid automatic public alerts until false-positive behavior and operational responsibility are addressed.

19. **Add a reproducible offline judge/demo path.** Bundle a small, clearly labeled real-data sample with a manifest (or clearly labeled synthetic fixture if licensing/data-size requires it), one-command setup, deterministic outputs, and automated scientific/API/UI tests. Include a short demo path that works without credentials, network-dependent model calls, or fragile third-party services.

20. **Add data citations, acknowledgements, and licensing at the point of use.** Identify the exact NASA FIRMS/LANCE products, versions, standard vs near-real-time status, and access date; distinguish NASA imagery served by GIBS from the active-fire records used in calculations. Check redistribution and attribution terms for FIRMS, GDELT, ReliefWeb, news snippets, administrative boundaries, and any derived dataset.

## P2 — Improve competition positioning with verifiable differentiation

21. **Use competitor repositories as a benchmark, not as evidence that their claims are true.** Public projects already emphasize reproducible data pipelines, overlap-period calibration, cell-day presence, uncertainty/provenance, offline demos, and automated tests. Flare-Chronos additionally publishes benchmark charts and reported paired-sensor FRP experiments; independently reproduce or challenge such claims on a held-out dataset and explain what your approach adds. Project Icarus’s deterministic science layer, cell-day aggregation, provenance, and explicit synthetic-data labeling are useful patterns to adopt. [Flare-Chronos](https://github.com/the-aris-club/flare-chronos) · [Project Icarus](https://github.com/mdhabibullahmahmudncs13/Icarus-Harmonization-of-MODIS-and-VIIRS-Hot-Spots) · [FireLens evidence workbench](https://github.com/feng-ship/firelens-evidence)

22. **Make the novelty measurable and judgeable.** State one concise research question, one main metric, one baseline, and one result reproduced from data. A defensible modest result with uncertainty and a clear audit trail will be stronger than a menu of unvalidated corrections, broad “zero-hallucination” claims, or speculative global performance.

23. **Verify all community and competitor references.** Link the exact NASA Space Apps discussions claimed as `#1786` and `#1879`, quote them accurately, and distinguish participant opinions from official requirements. Likewise, only name a competitor or attribute a technique after linking to a public repository or artifact that demonstrates it.
